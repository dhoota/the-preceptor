## Notes written

- rn-c11-2-5: Canadian child welfare laws give every person, nurses included, a duty to report suspected maltreatment to the local child welfare agency or police. Agency names vary by province.
- rn-c11-3-5: Tramadol is listed in Schedule I of the Controlled Drugs and Substances Act and is handled under controlled drug rules.
- rn-c11-4-2: SI units only, urea rather than BUN. Sodium 133 mmol/L and urea 9.3 mmol/L set against the Medical Council of Canada ranges.
- rn-c11-4-3: Urea 9.3 mmol/L is above the Medical Council of Canada range of 2.1 to 8.0 mmol/L.
- rn-c11-4-5: The 1 hour and 4 hour limits are US CMS rules. Canadian seclusion and restraint rules come from provincial mental health laws and hospital policy and vary by province.
- rn-c11-5-4: Disclosure rules vary by province. Ontario's PHIPA s. 40(1) permits disclosure to eliminate or reduce a significant risk of serious bodily harm.
- rn-c11-5-5: The Canadian Invega Sustenna monograph states doses as paliperidone, so the first dose is 150 mg, then 100 mg on day 8. Both go into the deltoid. The key stands.
- rn-c11-5-6: Assistive personnel are unregulated care providers in Canada. Rules for teaching, assigning and supervising them come from each provincial nursing regulator.

## Other checks

- The CDSA text, current to 2026-09-03, lists tramadol as item 27 of Schedule I. The note gives no start date because the in-force date was not confirmed from a primary source.
- The Invega Sustenna monograph was revised September 27, 2023. It gives 150 mg on day 1 and 100 mg on day 8, both in the deltoid, with monthly doses in the deltoid or gluteal muscle after that.
- MCC ranges read on the page: sodium 136 to 146 mmol/L, urea 2.1 to 8.0 mmol/L, urine specific gravity 1.005 to 1.030. The page was last updated December 2020.
- Ontario's Mental Health Act s. 53 sets out how restraint is documented. It has no 1 hour or 4 hour rule. The note names no province and says the rules vary by province.
- The CWRP page says every person in Canada has a duty to report known or suspected child maltreatment. Professionals who work with children have an added responsibility.
- PHIPA s. 40(1) reads that a custodian may disclose when it believes on reasonable grounds that disclosure is necessary to eliminate or reduce a significant risk of serious bodily harm.
