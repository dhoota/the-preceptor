## Notes written

- rn-s28-20: Glucose in mmol/L only (6.2 mmol/L), and the Diabetes Canada hypoglycemia threshold of below 4.0 mmol/L.
- rn-s28-24: CNPS lists suspected child abuse or neglect and a risk of serious harm as limits to a youth's confidentiality. The governing laws vary by province.
- rn-s28-32: Adult protection laws differ by province. The Newfoundland and Labrador act counts a substantial loss of assets as abuse. The Nova Scotia act does not name financial abuse.
- rn-s28-44: Each province and territory runs its own death investigation system under a chief coroner or chief medical examiner.
- rn-s28-49: TRC Call to Action 22 asks the Canadian health system to recognize Indigenous healing practices and use them with healers and Elders on request.

## Other checks

- The NS act defines abuse as physical abuse, sexual abuse and mental cruelty, plus neglect. It does not name financial abuse. Section 5 requires every person to report. The NL act defines abuse to include substantial loss of assets. Section 12 requires reporting.
- The StatCan page confirms 13 provincial and territorial Chief Coroners and Chief Medical Examiners. Which provinces use a medical examiner (Alberta, Manitoba, Nova Scotia, Newfoundland and Labrador) was cross-checked on Wikipedia but is not named in the note.
- The TRC Call 22 wording was read in the PDF text. It says "Aboriginal patients", and the note paraphrases this as Indigenous clients to follow house style.
