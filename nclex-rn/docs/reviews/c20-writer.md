## Points for the reviewer

- rn-c20-2-5 uses the Irish Medication Safety Network for the peripheral potassium limits because I could not find a free US source with the same explicit numbers. The item keeps to 40 mEq/L and 5 mEq/hour, which is within both US and Irish practice. A US nursing text could replace or join this source.
- rn-c20-2-4 lists "a chest x-ray" and "a blood transfusion" as distractor requests. They are plausible but weak. Check that they are not strawmen.
- rn-c20-3-3 is tagged MOC because it tests which findings to report first. It could also be read as PA.
- rn-c20-3-6 is tagged MOC because of the referral blank. The first blank is a liver trend judgment.
- rn-c20-4-5: the vancomycin reaction is keyed to "Stop the vancomycin infusion" first. Some local policies say to slow the infusion instead. The stem says the bag ran much faster than prescribed, which supports stopping it first.
- rn-c20-4-6 teaches that tolerance falls during a hospital stay. The SAMHSA toolkit supports lower tolerance after a period of not using. Check the wording against the toolkit.
- rn-c20-5-2 marks the home health aide with 2 documented varicella vaccine doses as "may continue usual contact". This follows CDC evidence of immunity. Covering the lesions is still advised.
- rn-c20-5-5 has no option to give naloxone or call emergency services. The client follows commands with a respiratory rate of 12/minute and SpO2 of 94%, so a same-day report to the provider is keyed. Confirm this is not a second defensible action.
- Difficulty covers only 3 levels (2, 3 and 4). The bank gate for case batches does not check difficulty spread.
- The whole-bank near-duplicate test fails at times on pairs that do not involve c20, such as rn-c02-1-1 with rn-c18-5-1. Other writers are working at the same time. No c20 stem appeared in a flagged pair after I reworded rn-c20-1-1. I did not touch other batches.
