## How the claims were checked

- Restraints: the Ontario Act s. 4 to 8 lets a hospital restrain only to prevent serious bodily harm or under a consented plan of treatment, and requires hospital policies and monitoring "in accordance with the regulations". It has no 1 hour face-to-face rule.
- Delegation: CNPS (revised February 2024) says the delegating nurse "is ultimately responsible for the decision to delegate" and cites separate standards from several provincial regulators.
- PHIPA s. 38(3): a facility may disclose presence, general health status and location if it offers the individual the option to object at the first reasonable opportunity and the individual does not.
- PHIPA s. 43(1)(g): disclosure to a person carrying out an inspection or investigation "authorized by a warrant ... for the purpose of complying with the warrant".
- PHIPA s. 12(1): a custodian "shall take steps that are reasonable in the circumstances" to protect information "against theft, loss and unauthorized use or disclosure".
- PHIPA s. 55(1), (3): correction requests are made in writing. The custodian replies no later than 30 days after receipt or extends the deadline for not more than 30 days. The item's rationale (60 days) is the HIPAA rule and was left as written.
- Canada Health Act s. 2: insured services are hospital, physician and surgical-dental services, and hospital services include drugs only "when administered in the hospital".
- Nursing Act, 1991: s. 11(1) restricts "registered practical nurse" to members. Section 6 continues the College of Nurses of Ontario.
- Licensure: the CNO INL page says "Nurses must be registered in a Canadian jurisdiction (province or territory) to practice there" and that registrants must follow CNO standards when caring for clients in Ontario.
- HCCA s. 20(1): paragraph 1 is the guardian of the person with authority over the treatment. A parent is paragraph 5.
- Human Rights Code s. 1: equal treatment in services without discrimination because of sexual orientation.
- Mental health: Ontario's Mental Health Act defines the substitute decision maker and treatment by reference to the HCCA. British Columbia's Bill 32 repealed the deemed consent clause in s. 31(1) effective December 3, 2025, but s. 8 still lets a director "sign consent to treatment forms" for a detained client. The note says the director "may authorize psychiatric treatment", which matches s. 8 and s. 31.
- Palliative care: the Health Canada Framework (December 4, 2018) says individual provinces and territories work from their own strategies and "there are differences across the country in how people receive palliative care".
- Ostomy: the Ontario ADP page (updated February 18, 2026) covers permanent colostomies and gives $975 per ostomy each year.
- Potassium: the MCC list gives 3.5 to 5.1 mmol/L in SI units.
