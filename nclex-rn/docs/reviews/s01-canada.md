## How the claims were checked

- Unregulated care providers: CNPS says the delegating nurse "is ultimately responsible for the decision to delegate". It cites separate standards from BC, Manitoba, Ontario, Alberta, Newfoundland and Labrador, and New Brunswick.
- RPN: the 2025 CNO Scope of Practice standard names Registered Practical Nurses (RPNs) as Ontario's practical nurse category.
- Substitute decision makers: CNPS says "Statutes tend to provide a hierarchy", with "first priority" going to a court-appointed decision maker or "the attorney for personal care". It lists a separate statute for each province, including Alberta's and Nova Scotia's Personal Directives Acts. That is why the note says "generally".
- Privacy laws: the OPC page lists PHIPA 2004 (Ontario) and the Health Information Act (Alberta) as provincial health records laws.
- Lab values: the MCC list (updated December 2020) gives hemoglobin as 115 to 155 g/L for females and 125 to 170 g/L for males, and glucose in mmol/L. The Diabetes Canada chapter defines hypoglycemia as below 4.0 mmol/L in people treated with insulin or an insulin secretagogue.
- Do Not Use list: the 2025 ISMP Canada bulletin lists OD and QD, with "Use daily" as the fix.
- Disclosure and apology: CNPS says "Some provinces have enacted legislation requiring the disclosure" and names the Healthcare Excellence Canada Canadian Disclosure Guidelines. The apology InfoLAW says "Yukon is the only Canadian jurisdiction without an Apology Act". Some provinces put this protection in other statutes, so the note says "apology legislation".
- Minors: the CMPA says capacity is judged case by case and that "In Québec, the age of consent is generally 14". The note says "most provinces" because New Brunswick sets an age by statute.
- Incident analysis: the Healthcare Excellence Canada page says the framework "is based on the 2006 Canadian Root Cause Analysis Framework". The PDF gives the 2012 citation.
- Organ donation: the TGLN page says designated hospitals under the Gift of Life Act must notify TGLN. The Nova Scotia page describes deemed consent at 19 and older under the Human Organ and Tissue Donation Act.
- Narcotics: Narcotic Control Regulations section 63(c) says a person in charge of a hospital must report any loss or theft of narcotics "within 10 days of his discovery thereof". The regulations list hydromorphone.
- Goals of Care Designation: AHS describes Goals of Care Designations as medical orders.

## Left empty on purpose

- rn-s01-03 and rn-s01-30 (interpreters): both items cite the US rule 45 CFR 92.201, and Canada has no federal equivalent. WebSearch had run out and no Canadian interpreter guidance could be reached with WebFetch, so no note was written. These two should get a follow-up check.
- rn-s01-44 (medication reconciliation) and rn-s01-50 (client identifiers): both are Accreditation Canada Required Organizational Practices. The ROP handbook could not be reached to confirm this, and the practice itself is the same. These are left empty.
- rn-s01-02: the glucose value is already given in mmol/L, and the key turns on SpO2.
- The other empty items (06, 07, 09, 10, 12, 13, 14, 16, 17, 18, 20, 22, 24, 25, 27, 28, 34, 35, 37, 38, 39, 42, 43, 45, 47, 48, 49) have no material Canadian difference.
