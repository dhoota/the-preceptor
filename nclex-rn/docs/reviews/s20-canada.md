## How the claims were checked

- Lab units: the MCC list (updated December 2020) gives sodium 136 to 146 mmol/L, potassium 3.5 to 5.1 mmol/L, bicarbonate 21 to 32 mmol/L, hemoglobin in g/L, platelets 130 to 380 x 10^9/L, absolute neutrophils 2.0 to 7.5 x 10^9/L and arterial PCO2 32 to 45 mm Hg. For monovalent ions, 1 mEq equals 1 mmol. 500 cells/mm3 equals 0.5 x 10^9/L.
- TST: chapter 4 of the Canadian Tuberculosis Standards (canada.ca) says reading "should be performed 48 to 72 hours after administration" and "Do not record erythema". Table 1 sets 10 mm or more for "any population considered at low risk of disease". It also says "A two-step TST should be performed if subsequent TSTs will be conducted at regular intervals (e.g., among health care or correctional workers)", with the second step 1 to 4 weeks later. The key (8 mm is negative) holds.
- Newborn screening: published Canadian reviews (for example PMC7510222 on Alberta) state that newborn screening is under provincial jurisdiction and panels are not harmonized. The NSO page says a sample "should be collected between 24 and 48 hours of age" and that a repeat is required after 24 hours when a sample is taken earlier. The key (arrange a repeat screen) holds.
- GBS: SOGC No. 298 (2013, reaffirmed August 2018, PMID 30103891) offers screening "at 35 to 37 weeks' gestation" with one swab to the vagina and then the rectum. PubMed shows no newer SOGC GBS guideline. The 2022 SOGC Retirement Notice No. 2 retired only the 2004 version. The JOGC full text returned HTTP 403, so the recommendation wording was confirmed through search result extracts.
- FIT: the CPAC 2023 to 2024 scan states "All jurisdictions use the fecal immunochemical test (FIT)." BC Cancer states "There are no dietary or medication restrictions" for the FIT. The note does not change the guaiac teaching in the key.
