## Notes written

- rn-s30-01, 03 and 45: Canada has no licensure compact. A nurse registers in each province or territory where care is given.
- rn-s30-04: scope is checked against provincial or territorial legislation and regulator standards, which vary by province.
- rn-s30-07: suspected measles is reported to the local medical officer of health under provincial law. Measles is also nationally notifiable to PHAC.
- rn-s30-09: the CDC list does not apply. Provincial and territorial laws set reportable diseases, and PHAC keeps a separate national list.
- rn-s30-10: HIPAA does not apply. Provincial health information laws or PIPEDA govern, and a relative still needs the client's consent.
- rn-s30-20: GINA does not apply. The Genetic Non-Discrimination Act bars requiring genetic tests or results for contracts, including life and disability insurance.
- rn-s30-24: glucose in mmol/L only.
- rn-s30-33: provincial law sets the substitute decision maker order. Ontario ranks a spouse or partner ahead of children.
- rn-s30-43: outbreak reporting duties come from provincial public health laws.
- rn-s30-44: an internationally educated nurse registers with the provincial or territorial regulator.
- rn-s30-46: provincial legislatures and regulators, and the CNA Code of Ethics for Nurses.
- rn-s30-47: potassium in mmol/L, numerically equal to mEq/L.
