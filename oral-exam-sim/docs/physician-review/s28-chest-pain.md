### chest-pain-16: Pressure while jogging

A 47-year-old man presents to the emergency department at 07:15 with 40 minutes of central chest pressure that began while he was jogging. It spreads to his left arm and he feels nauseated. He smokes one pack a day and has untreated dyslipidemia. He takes no medications and has no known heart disease. He is pale and diaphoretic. Heart sounds are normal and the lungs are clear. The hospital has a catheterization laboratory on site.

- Temperature: 36.8°C oral
- Pulse: 94/minute
- Resp.: 20/minute
- BP: 146/88 mmHg
- O2 sat: 97% on room air
- Weight: 88 kg

#### Q1
**Which of the following features of his history most increases the likelihood that his pain is ischemic?**

- A. Absence of prior cardiac disease
- B. Age younger than 50 years
- C. Exertional onset with arm radiation  **(key)**
- D. Nausea without vomiting
- E. Normal heart and lung examination

*Explanation:* Pain that starts with exertion and spreads to the arm carries some of the highest likelihood ratios for ACS in the pain history. Nausea is common in ACS but is nonspecific and adds little. A normal examination and no prior heart disease do not lower the probability meaningfully. Age under 50 lowers the baseline risk a little but does not outweigh an exertional history in a smoker with dyslipidemia.

*Key feature:* Chest pain 3. Take a detailed pain and symptom history to separate dangerous from benign causes and shape the differential.

*Source:* Gulati M and colleagues. 2021 AHA/ACC guideline for the evaluation and diagnosis of chest pain. Circulation. 2021.

#### Q2
*Update:* The ECG shows 2 mm upsloping ST depression at the J point in V1 to V5 running into tall, symmetrical T waves, with 1 mm ST elevation in aVR. Potassium is 4.1 mmol/L.

**Which of the following is the most likely explanation for these ECG findings?**

- A. Acute proximal LAD occlusion  **(key)**
- B. Benign early repolarization
- C. Hyperkalemia with peaked T waves
- D. Left ventricular hypertrophy with strain
- E. Posterior wall myocardial infarction

*Explanation:* Upsloping ST depression at the J point that runs into tall symmetrical precordial T waves is the de Winter pattern, a sign of acute proximal LAD occlusion. His potassium of 4.1 mmol/L argues against hyperkalemia. Strain from hypertrophy gives downsloping ST depression with inverted T waves in the lateral leads. Posterior infarction gives horizontal ST depression mainly in V1 to V3 with tall R waves. Early repolarization causes ST elevation, not depression.

*Key feature:* Chest pain 1. In undifferentiated chest pain, first consider and quickly recognize lethal causes like ACS, PE, pneumothorax, and aortic dissection.

*Source:* de Winter RJ, Verouden NJ, Wellens HJ and colleagues. A new ECG sign of proximal LAD occlusion. N Engl J Med. 2008. https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMc0804737

#### Q3
**Which of the following is the most appropriate management now?**

- A. Early angiography within 24 hours
- B. Fibrinolysis with tenecteplase
- C. Immediate primary PCI  **(key)**
- D. Repeat ECG and troponin in 1 hour
- E. Telemetry admission and serial troponins

*Explanation:* The de Winter pattern with ongoing ischemic pain is treated as a STEMI equivalent, and this hospital can do primary PCI now. Angiography within 24 hours is the plan for high-risk NSTE-ACS without ongoing ischemia, not for an occluded artery. Fibrinolysis is for STEMI when timely PCI is not available. Waiting for troponins or a repeat ECG delays reperfusion while myocardium is lost.

*Key feature:* Chest pain 1. In undifferentiated chest pain, first consider and quickly recognize lethal causes like ACS, PE, pneumothorax, and aortic dissection.

*Source:* Byrne RA and colleagues. 2023 ESC guidelines for the management of acute coronary syndromes. Eur Heart J. 2023. https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/44/38/3720/7243210

*Explanation:* Exertional pressure that continues at rest, spreads to the shoulder and comes with sweating and new ST depression in V4 to V6 is an acute coronary syndrome until proven otherwise.
