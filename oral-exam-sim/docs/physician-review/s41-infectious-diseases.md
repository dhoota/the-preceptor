He has never had a blood transfusion. He has had no cough, vomiting or diarrhea. His immunizations are up to date. He is irritable but consolable. Capillary refill is 2 seconds. His chest is clear, with no increased work of breathing. His abdomen is soft and the spleen is not palpable. There is no joint swelling and no bone tenderness.

- Temperature: 39.6°C rectal
- Pulse: 142/minute
- Resp.: 28/minute
- BP: 98/58 mmHg
- O2 sat: 98% on room air

#### Q1
**Which of the following best explains why this child is at high risk of overwhelming bacterial infection?**

- A. Functional asplenia from splenic infarction  **(key)**
- B. Hypogammaglobulinemia from folate deficiency
- C. Iron overload from repeated transfusion
- D. Lymphopenia from hydroxyurea therapy
- E. Neutropenia caused by penicillin prophylaxis

*Explanation:* In HbSS, repeated splenic infarction causes functional asplenia in early childhood, which fits a spleen that is no longer palpable at age 4. Loss of splenic function leaves him open to rapidly fatal infection with encapsulated organisms such as Streptococcus pneumoniae. He has never been transfused, so iron overload does not apply. Prophylactic penicillin lowers his pneumococcal risk and nothing in the case suggests neutropenia. He does not take hydroxyurea, and he takes folic acid, which does not cause antibody deficiency.

*Key feature:* Infectious diseases 3. Identify host factors, like immunosuppression, age, comorbidity, travel, or substance use, that raise the risk of severe infection.

### infectious-diseases-17: Jaw swelling after a toothache

Since this morning he has had trouble swallowing and his voice sounds thick. He has type 2 diabetes treated with metformin. He is sitting upright and leaning forward, drooling into a basin. There is firm, tender swelling of both submandibular regions that extends into the upper neck. The floor of the mouth is raised and woody, pushing the tongue up and back. He has no stridor. Capillary glucose is 18.2 mmol/L.

- Temperature: 38.9°C oral
- Pulse: 118/minute
- Resp.: 22/minute

#### Q1
**Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis for this man?**

- A. Acute sialadenitis
- B. Ludwig angina  **(key)**
- C. Peritonsillar abscess
- D. Retropharyngeal abscess
- E. Submandibular lymphadenitis

*Explanation:* Bilateral firm submandibular swelling, a raised woody floor of the mouth and an upward displaced tongue after a lower molar infection define Ludwig angina. Diabetes is a recognized risk factor, and a lower molar is the usual dental source. Sialadenitis and lymphadenitis are usually one sided and do not lift the floor of the mouth. A peritonsillar abscess causes a bulging tonsillar pillar and uvular deviation. A retropharyngeal abscess causes posterior pharyngeal swelling rather than floor of mouth induration.

*Key feature:* Infectious diseases 4. Look beyond an apparently minor infection for complications, such as peritonsillar abscess, necrotizing fasciitis, or PID.

*Source:* Bridwell R, Gottlieb M, Koyfman A, Long B. Diagnosis and management of Ludwig's angina: an evidence-based review. Am J Emerg Med. 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33383265/

- C. CT neck before airway planning

- E. Rapid sequence intubation now

*Explanation:* Drooling, a thick voice and a tongue pushed upward warn of impending airway obstruction even without stridor. The airway should be planned early with anesthesia and ENT, often as awake fibreoptic intubation with a surgical airway ready. Rapid sequence induction with paralysis risks a failed airway because the anatomy is distorted. Lying him flat for CT before an airway plan can precipitate obstruction. Nebulized epinephrine does not shrink infected tissue, and early Ludwig angina is often firm cellulitis without a collection to drain at the bedside.

### infectious-diseases-47: Fever, rash and sore throat in a young man

A 27-year-old man presents to the emergency department with 5 days of fever, sore throat, aching muscles and a rash. He has had loose stools for 2 days. Three weeks ago he had condomless anal sex with a new male partner. He has never taken pre-exposure prophylaxis. His last HIV test, 8 months ago, was negative. He has no medical history. He has a faint pink maculopapular rash on his trunk, two shallow painful ulcers inside his lower lip and small tender lymph nodes in his neck and armpits. His pharynx is red without exudate. - Pulse: 104/minute
- Resp.: 16/minute
- BP: 118/72 mmHg
- O2 sat: 99% on room air
- Weight: 74 kg

#### Q1
**Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis in this man?**

- A. Acute HIV infection  **(key)**
- B. Infectious mononucleosis
- C. Secondary syphilis
- D. Streptococcal pharyngitis
- E. Toxic shock syndrome

*Explanation:* Fever, sore throat, a truncal rash, painful mouth ulcers, diffuse lymph nodes and diarrhea 3 weeks after a condomless exposure fit acute HIV infection. It is easily mistaken for a viral illness if a sexual history is not taken. Secondary syphilis usually appears weeks after a chancre and often involves the palms and soles. Mononucleosis rarely causes mucosal ulcers and diarrhea. The negative antigen test and absent exudate argue against streptococcal pharyngitis, and he is not in shock.

*Key feature:* Infectious diseases 5. Include infection, even uncommon ones like malaria, TB, endocarditis, or Lyme disease, in vague presentations such as confusion.

*Source:* Tan DHS, Hull MW, Onyegbule SO, et al. Canadian guideline on HIV pre- and postexposure prophylaxis: 2025 update. CMAJ. 2025. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12680393/

#### Q2
**Which of the following is the most appropriate test for the likely diagnosis?**

- A. CD4 lymphocyte count
- B. Heterophile antibody test
- C. HIV antibody only rapid test
- D. HIV antigen and antibody test  **(key)**
- E. Western blot for HIV

*Explanation:* A fourth generation HIV antigen and antibody test detects p24 antigen before antibodies form, so it is the preferred test, and people with signs of acute HIV should also have an HIV RNA test. Antibody only rapid tests and Western blot can be negative this early. A CD4 count does not diagnose HIV. A heterophile test looks for mononucleosis.

*Key feature:* Infectious diseases 7. Tailor fever investigations to age and context, avoiding both excessive and insufficient testing.
