## Passes with notes (no fix required)

- arrhythmia-48 q4 (writer flag, opioid and general anesthesia for a CPVT storm). Adjudicated: the key holds. Bellamy 2019 (full text read) concludes that epinephrine should be avoided and that "the optimum therapies available are IV opiates and general anesthetics". ESC ACS 2023 also lists sedation or general anaesthesia to reduce sympathetic drive (IIb) for recurrent life-threatening VA. Every distractor is harmful or ineffective, so only one answer is defensible. Optional improvement: the last sentence says a beta blocker "follows once she is stable". IV beta blockade (propranolol or esmolol) and flecainide are also used acutely in CPVT storm, so "with IV beta blockade" could be added. Not a defect of the key.
- arrhythmia-48 q1 to q3: Abbas 2022 (read) confirms a normal resting ECG and the exercise test as the most useful diagnostic tool. The CPVT angle is new to the bank. The oral case athlete-exertional-syncope names CPVT only in its differential.
- arrhythmia-50 q4 (writer flag, ESC 2022 not opened). The key holds: an ICD is the protection against sudden death in short QT syndrome, and the Merck page (2026) supports it. The reviewer could not open the ESC 2022 ventricular arrhythmia guideline text either. Recommended, not required: cite Zeppenfeld K and colleagues, 2022 ESC Guidelines for the management of patients with ventricular arrhythmias and the prevention of sudden cardiac death, Eur Heart J 2022, as the primary source once the writer has read its short QT recommendations.
- arrhythmia-50 q2 and q3: the QT-shortening causes match Patel 2010.
- arrhythmia-51 q3 (writer flag, pacing over atropine in infranodal block). Adjudicated: the key holds. Pads are on, he is hypotensive and confused at 30/minute, and every clue places the block below the node. The 2018 ACC/AHA/HRS bradycardia guideline (Kusumoto FM and colleagues, Circulation 2019) limits atropine to AV block believed to be at the AV nodal level. The oral case complete-heart-block-pacing gives the same teaching ("Infranodal block with a wide escape usually does not respond"). The only cited source is a paramedic case report (Armour 2022), which supports the paradoxical-worsening sentence. Recommended, not required: add the ACC/AHA/HRS 2018 guideline as the source of the key.
- arrhythmia-51 q1, q2, q4: 2:1 block that cannot be graded, infranodal clues and flecainide as the cause are new to the bank. Timeline consistent (dose doubled 3 weeks ago, symptoms for 2 weeks).
- arrhythmia-52 q1: ESC 2023 text verified: "malignant ventricular arrhythmias occurring at the time of reperfusion do not confer poor prognosis, sustained VT or VF occurring during ongoing ischaemia or late after reperfusion (>48 h) is associated with an increase in long-term mortality".
- arrhythmia-52 q2 (menu) is a named repeat for physician judgement. Observing AIVR, premature beats and nonsustained VT after reperfusion is already keyed in arrhythmia-11 q2 and a q5 item in s03. It is the literal wording of key feature 7 ("Manage reperfusion rhythms mainly with support"), so it fits the decision D1 exception, and the menu adds new rhythms (Mobitz I, polymorphic VT, complete heart block with hypotension). ESC 2023 text verified: AIVR "does not require intervention", premature beats need "no specific therapy", and treatment of asymptomatic, haemodynamically irrelevant VA is not recommended (III C).
- arrhythmia-52 q3: Mobitz I after inferior reperfusion, observed, is new to the bank. ESC 2023 keeps chronotropic drugs for haemodynamic intolerance or high-degree block without a stable escape (verified).
