## For the physician

- Every source was opened, but for some only the abstract or a summary was readable: the SIS 2025 pediatric update (abstract and a University of Connecticut pharmacy summary), Wolfe and Wang (abstract) and the EAST BCVI guideline (abstract). The 2026 CCS/CPCA pediatric acute heart failure guideline could not be opened, so it is not cited.
- pediatric-fever-03 q2 keys piperacillin tazobactam alone as the antibiotic. The SIS 2025 update prefers it or ertapenem over ceftriaxone with metronidazole. Ertapenem, meropenem and ceftriaxone with metronidazole are left out of the options so no distractor is defensible. Please confirm this matches your practice.
- pediatric-fever-03 q4 keys epinephrine. The 2026 pediatric SSC guideline makes no recommendation between epinephrine and norepinephrine, so norepinephrine is not an option. The reasons against dobutamine and milrinone rest on their vasodilating action, not on a guideline statement. The 5 to 10 mL/kg aliquot rests on a narrative review, because the guidelines make no statement on known cardiac dysfunction.
- pediatric-fever-11 q2 uses the ACSM 2023 end point of about 38°C or recovery of mental status. The keeper environmental-01 keys removal at about 39°C. Both are accepted wording here, but please note the two sources differ. Its cooling list overlaps the keeper's, because key feature 2 is literally "cool rapidly with several methods" (decision D1).
- pediatric-fever-11 q1 keys a rectal temperature before cooling. ACSM advises this only when a rectal temperature can be taken at once, and says cooling comes first otherwise. The stem places him in the emergency department with a nurse at the bedside.
- pediatric-fever-12 follows the AAP 2010 clinical report for implants placed within 2 months (parenteral therapy for otitis media and meropenem with vancomycin for meningitis). There is no Canadian guideline on this. Doses come from the CPS meningitis statement.
- pediatric-fever-07 q4 accepts doxycycline for a 9-year-old. The CDC page gives the adult regimen only. Please confirm the pediatric regimen you would teach.
- multiple-trauma-06 q1 accepts cefazolin and tetanus for the amputation stump. These are standard open wound care and are not stated in the cited replantation abstract.
- multiple-trauma-08 q2 now keys the ventilation change rather than the cause of the end-tidal gap, to stay clear of the g09 rebuild of airway-09.
- multiple-trauma-10 q4 keys factor concentrate and tranexamic acid from a surgical guideline, since no trauma guideline for von Willebrand disease exists. Desmopressin is accepted only for a known responder.
