## Writer flags adjudicated

- anaphylaxis-48 q4, tryptase: WAO gives the rule as peak above 1.2 x baseline + 2. WAO prints the unit as "ng/L", a typographical slip for ng/mL. mcg/L equals ng/mL, so the units in the item are right. Arithmetic: 1.2 x 6.0 + 2 = 9.2 mcg/L, and 18.6 is about twice that. WAO places the peak at 1 to 2 hours and the baseline at least 24 hours after recovery, and the 2 day baseline meets that. Correct.
- anaphylaxis-49 q3, infant dosing: 0.01 mg/kg x 9 kg = 0.09 mg = 0.09 mL of 1 mg/mL, under WAO 2020. CSACI (Halbrich 2015) suggests the 0.15 mg autoinjector for children under 15 kg in the community. That device is not an option, so the key holds in an emergency department with ampoules.
- anaphylaxis-51 q2, infusion rate: the RCUK refractory algorithm PDF was read. It gives 1 mg in 100 mL (10 mcg/mL), a start of 0.5 to 1.0 mL/kg/hour, a dedicated line, no piggybacking, not on the cuff side, and notes that a large rise in BP suggests overdose. At 64 kg the range is 32 to 64 mL/hour, so 48 mL/hour fits. 16 mL/hour is 0.25 mL/kg/hour, and 160 and 480 mL/hour are 2.5 and 7.5 mL/kg/hour. The update names RCUK, so the key holds under that reference. No Canadian infusion guideline was found.
- anaphylaxis-50 q2, tilt-table extrapolation: Shmuely 2018 confirmed (median 2 versus 48 jerks, fewer than 10 favouring syncope, ages 15 and over). The explanation now names the study population. The key also rests on his hypotension and on the jerking stopping within seconds.
