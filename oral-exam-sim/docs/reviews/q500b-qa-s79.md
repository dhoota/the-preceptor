## Sources checked

- Corbeddu 2021, PMC8688849: opened. Supports onset at 2 to 4 months (often 6 to 12 weeks), biphasic stridor in subglottic disease, beard distribution in about 50%, frequent misdiagnosis as croup, endoscopy as the gold standard, and propranolol first line. It does not mention nebulized epinephrine. It does describe steroids used as a bridge in severe cases.
- CPS croup statement: the page would not load (bot check). The 2026 update giving a fixed 5 mL L-epinephrine dose is already confirmed in docs/reviews/conformance-s17.md and batch06.md, so I accept it.
- ESC 2019 PE: the cited facts (proximal DVT on CUS in 30 to 50%, echo NPV 40 to 50%, SBP below 90 mmHg for more than 15 minutes, no routine lysis at intermediate risk, anticoagulation during workup at high or intermediate probability) match the guideline.
- Mos 2014 (PubMed 24735976): confirmed. Wells, D-dimer and CTPA in 516 patients with suspected recurrent PE. An unlikely Wells score plus a normal D-dimer excluded PE with 0% events.
- Wittram 2004 (PubMed 15371604): confirmed. Chronic PE shows webs or flaps, recanalization and obtuse angles. Acute defects are central or form acute angles. The paper covers causes of misdiagnosis.
- Le Gal 2009 (PubMed 19228281): confirmed. REVERSE cohort. No significant change from baseline imaging safely excluded recurrent VTE.
- Soulaidopoulos 2018, PMC5871824: opened. A-a gradient of at least 15 mmHg (20 when older than 64), bubbles in the left atrium at beats 4 to 6, fewer than 3 beats means an intracardiac shunt, and transplant as the only established treatment. **It reports a garlic trial with reversal in 14 of 21 patients against 1 of 20, and conflicting pentoxifylline data.** See sob-51 q4.
- MSD Manual, Cheng, reviewed June 2026: opened. Supports every epiglottitis statement and ceftriaxone 50 to 75 mg/kg (maximum 2 g). Cefazolin and vancomycin are not on the page. What the item says about them is standard microbiology and is correct.

## Verdicts

### sob-48 Noisy breathing that keeps coming back: FAIL (1 item)

- q1 PASS. Biphasic stridor, onset at 8 weeks and a growing beard-area hemangioma make the key secure. Every distractor reason is true.
- q2 PASS.
- q3 FAIL (mock-mode cue, B6). The option "propranolol 5 mg PO" is relevant only to an infantile hemangioma. When every question shows at once it gives away the q1 key. Fix: replace it with a distractor that does not name the diagnosis and matches the dose and route format, for example "budesonide 2 mg nebulized". Then rewrite the last sentence of the explanation: drop the propranolol sentence and say why nebulized budesonide does not relieve obstruction within minutes. The writer's uncertainty 1 (epinephrine as a bridge) is acceptable. Nebulized epinephrine is standard temporizing care for acute upper airway narrowing, and the source names steroids, not propranolol, as the ED bridge. No option offers a steroid, so the key remains the single best answer. The fixed 5 mL CPS 2026 dose differs from signed sob-02 (0.5 mL/kg to a maximum of 5 mL, which is 2.7 mL at 5.4 kg). conformance-s17 already flagged this for the physician, so it is not a silent reversal. I note it here only.
- q4 PASS. Transfer to a centre with paediatric airway endoscopy and intensive care is defensible (writer's uncertainty 2).

### sob-49 Breathless two weeks after ankle surgery: FAIL (whole SAMP, rebuild)

Keep the false reassurance of a negative two-point CUS and a normal RV as the core. Replace q1, q3 and q4 with decisions the bank does not have. Examples: when to repeat a CUS in 1 week, V/Q against CTPA once transfer is possible, or what to do with an incidental subsegmental defect. Do not use "CUS first", "anticoagulate while waiting" or "SBP below 90 for 15 minutes" as a keyed decision. Put the new events in an order where no update names an earlier key. If any dalteparin dose stays, apply the 18 000 unit cap. Writer uncertainties 3 and 4 are moot after the rebuild. The apixaban statement is correct.
