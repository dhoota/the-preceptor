## For the physician

- abdominal-pain-24 q1. Goonewardene 2015 is behind a paywall I could not open. Its link to diverticulitis rests on the Chan, Dorfman and Chan 2014 abstract it cites (Acad Emerg Med 21 Suppl 1: S206), which reported sterile pyuria in 72.7% of diverticulitis cases. I found no guideline (WSES 2020 checked) that states it. The teaching point is standard, but its best support is a conference abstract.
- abdominal-pain-32 q1. The best primary study found is old: Scott 1983, 50 adults and 50 children. The key "Pyuria is common in appendicitis" rests on 12 of 50 children (24%). Please confirm "common" is acceptable.
- gi-bleed-22 q3. The only Canadian source for the 20 mL/kg bolus is the TREKK gastroenteritis bottom line, which is written for dehydration. The key is the same, but this child's picture is intussusception with shock.
- gi-bleed-32 q3. The paediatric evidence for octreotide is very low certainty (Kozak 2026 systematic review, 3 observational studies). The key follows adult practice and the Italian society paper. Please confirm.
- gi-bleed-42 q2. The older Romano 2017 paper says a maternal milk-free diet is "not necessarily indicated" in a thriving breastfed infant. The newer 2025 SIGENP and SIAIP position paper suggests a 2 to 4 week maternal elimination trial. The key follows the newer paper.
- headache-23 q3. Shunt series radiographs are keyed. The evidence on their yield in children is mixed: low sensitivity alone, and better only when combined with CT. Please confirm the menu key, or accept 2 of 3.
- headache-41 q3. No guideline exists for septic cavernous sinus thrombosis. The key (vancomycin with ceftriaxone) rests on a review of case reports.
- headache-43 q2. I could not find an open, verifiable source for C3 returning to normal by about 8 weeks, so I removed the claim. If you want it back, KDIGO 2021 did not state it in the text I searched.
