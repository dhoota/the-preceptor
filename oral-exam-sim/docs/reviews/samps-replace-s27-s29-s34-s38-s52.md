### Key and content findings

- **ems-27 q1 and SALT.** The rule in the explanation is correct SALT (Lerner 2008). An adult who is still not breathing after the airway is opened is triaged as dead. Rescue breaths are given only to children, and the pulse plays no part in that step. The SAMP did not apply this correctly. Its distractor used "pulseless" as the dead criterion, and the stem described patients with absent breathing. Strict SALT would tag those patients as dead, even though q2 and q4 of the same SAMP treat them. That is unfair when bag-mask ventilation and naloxone reliably rescue an apneic patient with a pulse. The stem now gives each of the four a slow rate, so the dead branch never applies. The pulseless distractor is now a tense, alert woman with a pulse of 130/minute, who passes every SALT check. The key, the man breathing 4/minute, is correct under SALT and under any other scheme.
- **ems-27 q2 overlap.** "Bag-valve-mask ventilation, then naloxone" repeated the q1 key of multiple-patients-31 (s44, six fentanyl patients from a shelter). q2 now covers the three drowsy patients breathing 10 to 12 times a minute. The Ontario ALS PCS 5.4 opioid directive was read in full: naloxone is for a respiratory rate below 10 or inadequate ventilation, and buprenorphine-naloxone is only for withdrawal with a COWS of 8 or more. So the new key is to monitor them and give naloxone if the rate falls below 10. q1 still prioritizes a hypoventilating patient, as multiple-patients-31 q2 does, but under field SALT rather than ED sequencing.
- **first-trimester-bleeding-34 q2: copy, rebuilt.** The old q2 keyed "pregnancy of unknown location" for an hCG of 1 450 IU/L with an empty scan. That is the keyed decision of signed-off first-trimester-bleeding-03 (hCG 1 100 IU/L) and of one more s38 item. Its point that a cervical source does not locate the pregnancy is the q3 key of signed-off first-trimester-bleeding-12, which is also postcoital bleeding at 8 weeks.
