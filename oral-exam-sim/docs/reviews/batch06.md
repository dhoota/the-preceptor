## bronchiolitis-apnea. A baby who went blue in the car seat

Verdict: Corrected.

Changes:

1. Stem triage level. Before: CTAS 2 with SpO2 86 percent, grunting and a witnessed apnea. After: CTAS 1. SpO2 under 90 percent with severe distress meets level 1.
2. Stem temperature. Added degrees C to every case stem in this batch, which lacked units.
3. Dexamethasone plus epinephrine feedback. Before: said it does not change admission. After: says the one Canadian trial that hinted at fewer admissions was not conclusive after adjustment and is not confirmed. The old wording misstated the trial.
4. Handover point on RSV. Before: vague advice on future seasons and sibling contacts. After: note she did not get RSV monoclonal antibody and flag it for her pediatrician.
5. Tube depth. Added the weight plus 6 cm rule and chest X ray confirmation.
6. Pertussis rubric. Added a pyloric stenosis warning for macrolides in early infancy. Moved its source from TREKK bronchiolitis, which does not cover pertussis, to a new PHAC pertussis source.
7. Citations. CPS bronchiolitis now says 2014, updated 2021, with URL. AHA updated from 2020 Part 4 to 2025 Part 8.

Verified: CPS oxygen threshold under 90 percent, no salbutamol, no routine epinephrine, no combined epinephrine and dexamethasone, isotonic IV fluid. Azithromycin 10 mg/kg daily for 5 days in infants under 6 months matches PHAC and CDC guidance. Rocuronium 4 mg, ketamine 4 to 8 mg and high flow at about 8 L/min are correct for 3.9 kg. Both gases are internally consistent.

Residual uncertainty: CPS says there is insufficient evidence for routine high flow. The case uses it as rescue, which is common practice. The PHAC pertussis URL came from search results and should be clicked once.

## severe-croup. Noisy breathing at 3 a.m.

Verdict: Corrected.

Changes:

1. Westley score. Before: a Westley of 6 was called severe. Westley 3 to 7 is moderate. After: the finding names the moderate band and the model answer grades severity on the CPS clinical descriptors, which do make this severe.
2. Epinephrine dose. Before: only the weight based 0.5 mL/kg to 5 mL. After: notes CPS uses a fixed 5 mL dose. Both give 5 mL here.
3. Dexamethasone cap. Added the TREKK maximum of 12 mg. Her 7.8 mg dose is well under it.
4. Observation. Before: at least 2 hours. After: 2 to 4 hours, as CPS states. Also fixed the claim that epinephrine lasts 1 to 2 hours to say its effect fades by about 2 hours.
5. Heliox. Before: offered as a bridge without caveat. After: states CPS does not recommend it routinely.
6. Tube depth. Before: 3 times the tube size, so 10.5 cm for a 3.5 mm tube. That is too shallow for a 2 year old and risks extubation. After: depth by age, about 13 cm, with a warning not to use 3 times a downsized tube.
7. Front of neck backup. Before: needle cricothyroidotomy as the usual rescue. Croup swelling sits at the cricoid ring, below the membrane, so a needle cric may not bypass it. After: smaller tube, bag mask with PEEP, supraglottic airway, then ENT for bronchoscopy or tracheostomy.
8. Sedation consequence. Before: heart rate fell to 110, which is normal for age. After: 80.
9. Admission wording softened to "more than one dose of epinephrine is a common reason to admit".
10. Citations. CPS title corrected to "Acute management of croup in the emergency department", 2017, updated 2026, with URL. TREKK URL added. AHA updated to 2025 Part 8.

Verified: dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg is 7.8 mg. Ketamine 13 to 26 mg. Predicted cuffed tube 4.0 mm at 2 years.

Residual uncertainty: the adult ICU choice is labelled partial. Some examiners would call it unsafe.

## pediatric-dka. A tired 9 year old who keeps vomiting

Verdict: Corrected.

Changes:

1. Dehydration estimate. Before: 10 percent "by weight loss". Her 3 kg loss over 2 months is mostly tissue. After: assume 7 to 10 percent in severe DKA and explain why weight loss overstates it.
2. Corrected sodium. Before: about 137. After: 137 to 139, depending on the formula. ISPAD's factor of 2 gives 139.
3. Cerebral edema risk factors. Before: listed young age as one of hers. She is 9. After: her factors are listed, with age under 5 named as a factor she does not have. Rubric text updated to match.
4. Large bolus feedback. Before: implied PECARN supports fast fluid. After: the fast PECARN arm used one 20 mL/kg bolus, not 60 mL/kg. Mentions hyperchloremia.
5. Hypokalemia. Before: consider holding insulin if potassium falls under 3.5. After: increase potassium, and lower the insulin rate rather than stopping it.
6. Intubation choice. Before: "hyperventilated to a pCO2 of 25", which is actually relative hypoventilation for a child at pCO2 14 and did not match the consequence node. After: intubated and set a standard rate. Feedback explains the pH crash and that you must match the pre intubation pCO2.
7. Citations. First authors added to ISPAD 2022 (Glaser) and Diabetes Canada 2018 (Wherrett) with the Diabetes Canada URL.

Verified: bolus 270 mL. Insulin 1.35 to 2.7 units/h without a bolus, started 1 hour after fluids. Dextrose at 14 to 17 mmol/L. Mannitol 13.5 to 27 g and 3 percent saline 68 to 135 mL over 10 to 15 minutes. Reduce fluids by a third. Anion gap 28 and the gas add up. The falling corrected sodium at 4 hours supports the edema story.

Residual uncertainty: Diabetes Canada favours replacement over 48 hours. The case accepts 24 to 48 hours per ISPAD.

## pediatric-status-epilepticus. A baby who will not stop shaking

Verdict: Needs physician attention. Corrected, with a legal framing change for sign off.

Changes:

1. Levetiracetam maximum. Before: 4500 mg. After: CPS maximum 3000 mg, with ESETT's 4500 mg named. Infusion time changed to 5 to 15 minutes per CPS. Dose of 510 mg is unaffected.
2. Fosphenytoin. Added the CPS maximum of 1000 mg PE and the 5 to 10 minute rate.
3. Phenobarbital. Added the CPS note that it may be the best second line drug under 6 months.
4. Valproate. Added mitochondrial disease and that IV valproate is only available in Canada through Special Access.
5. Pyridoxine. Added the CPS step of pyridoxine 100 mg IV under 18 months if seizures persist.
6. Hypertonic saline. Kept 3 to 5 mL/kg, maximum 100 mL, and noted that some pediatric protocols use 2 mL/kg boluses.
7. Tube size. Before: cuffed 3.5 mm. After: cuffed 3.0 or 3.5 mm. AHA sizing gives 3.0 under 1 year.
8. End node sodium. Before: 129 at 24 hours, a rise of 11 that breaks the case's own limit of about 8. After: 126.
9. CYFSA framing. Before: the strong answer was to consult CAS about whether to report, with "poverty alone is not neglect". Section 125(1) paragraph 1 covers physical harm "resulting from" a caregiver's "failure to adequately care for, provide for" the child, whatever the cause. This infant had status epilepticus from diluted formula. After: the strong answer is a personal report to CAS, told openly to the mother and framed as a request for help. Poverty is still named as not being neglect. Choice feedback, the social work node, the rubric text and the teaching were updated to match.
10. Citations. CPS statement given authors, year and URL. CYFSA URL added.

Verified: intranasal midazolam 1.7 mg is 0.2 mg/kg. Lorazepam 0.85 mg. Fosphenytoin 170 mg PE. Ketamine 8.5 to 17 mg. Rocuronium 8.5 mg. Osmolality 244 matches the calculated value. The urine findings fit water intoxication.

Residual uncertainty: the reporting threshold in poverty related harm is a judgment call. Please confirm the report is the expected strong answer. Also confirm your preferred 3 percent saline bolus volume for infants.

## pediatric-septic-shock. A limp toddler with a fever

Verdict: Corrected.

Changes:

1. Ceftriaxone. Before: "100 mg/kg once, maximum 4 g per day". CPS gives 100 mg/kg/day divided every 12 hours, maximum 4 g per day. After: both 50 mg/kg every 12 hours and a 100 mg/kg first dose are accepted.
2. Low dose choice. Before: said 50 mg/kg was below the meningitic dose. It is the CPS regimen. After: the feedback says the dose is acceptable and the gap is vancomycin. The consequence node now adds vancomycin and the next ceftriaxone dose at 12 hours, instead of a second 50 mg/kg dose 20 minutes later.
3. Vancomycin. Interval of every 6 hours added.
4. Meningococcal prophylaxis. Before: ceftriaxone 125 mg under 12 years and 250 mg from 12. Ontario uses under 15 and 15 and over. Ciprofloxacin is listed by Ontario for adults only, and the teaching now says so.
5. Gas. Before: bicarbonate 13 at pH 7.18 and pCO2 30. The calculated value is about 11. Changed to 11.
6. Calcium. The 2026 sepsis guideline found too little evidence to set a calcium target. Correction is now "consider". Added flushing the IO line between calcium and ceftriaxone.
7. Dopamine wording now attributes the preference for epinephrine or norepinephrine to Surviving Sepsis guidance since 2020.
8. Citations. Surviving Sepsis updated to the 2026 pediatric guideline. CPS meningitis title corrected to "older than 2 months", 2020, reaffirmed 2026, with URL. Ontario protocol given its year and PDF URL.

Verified: bolus 140 to 280 mL. D10W 28 to 70 mL. Calcium gluconate 7 mL. Ketamine and rocuronium 14 mg. Cuffed 4.0 to 4.5 mm tube. Rifampin 10 mg/kg twice daily for 2 days, maximum 600 mg. Staff prophylaxis only after intensive unprotected contact. Droplet precautions until 24 hours of effective therapy.

Residual uncertainty: I could not read the full 2026 Surviving Sepsis text. I checked only the summary. Hydrocortisone 2 mg/kg, maximum 100 mg, is common practice but is not a guideline dose.

## infant-nonaccidental-injury. A fussy baby who cries when changed

Verdict: Corrected.

Changes:

1. Analgesia. Before: morphine 0.05 to 0.1 mg/kg or intranasal fentanyl 1.5 mcg/kg in a 4 month old. Many Canadian protocols limit intranasal fentanyl to older children. After: acetaminophen about 90 mg and titrated IV morphine about 0.3 mg on a monitor, splinting, and no ibuprofen under 6 months.
2. TEN 4 FACESp wording. Before: "under 4.99 months". After: "4.99 months or younger". Also notes that ear and torso bruises are positive at any age under 4.
3. Place of safety. Before: a worker or police "can take a child". After: under the CYFSA a child protection worker, or a police officer, can bring a child to a place of safety without a warrant when there is substantial risk. The teaching now says the physician has no legal power to detain.
4. Citation. CYFSA source now names section 81 as well as 125, with URL.

Verified: section 125 threshold, the ongoing duty, the rule that the duty cannot be delegated, and the liability protection unless malicious or without reasonable grounds. Skeletal survey with repeat at about 2 weeks. Head imaging under 6 months. Skeletal survey for household contacts under 2 years. Pierce 2021 and Christian 2015 confirmed on PubMed.

Residual uncertainty: I could not load the statute text for section 81 directly. The warrantless power in 81(7) and the police power are confirmed by secondary sources only.

## Batch summary

- All 9 cases were corrected. One needs physician attention (status epilepticus, for the CYFSA reporting framing).
- Most serious clinical errors:
  - The croup tube depth of 10.5 cm for a 2 year old.
  - Needle cricothyroidotomy as the rescue for subglottic croup.
  - Ceftriaxone feedback that called the CPS dose too low.
  - The Ontario ceftriaxone prophylaxis age cutoff, which is 15, not 12.
  - The DKA intubation choice that contradicted its own consequence.
  - A 24 hour sodium rise that broke the case's own limit.
- Levetiracetam now uses the CPS maximum of 3000 mg.
- Citations are updated to AHA 2025 and Surviving Sepsis 2026. CPS titles and dates were fixed. Only confirmed URLs were added.
- The type check and the batch case tests pass.
