## Rework after Lens A

I kept every fix recorded in `docs/reviews/samps-s47-lensA.md`. I searched `src/samps/` before each rewrite. Ids, key feature counts and the batch gates are unchanged.

### Whole SAMPs rewritten

- pre-eclampsia-23 (key feature 3, three questions). This replaces the ruptured liver hematoma, which repeated s15 pre-eclampsia-07. The new case is HELLP presenting as a flu-like illness at 33 weeks, while her child is sick. It teaches three points from ACOG PB 222:
  - HELLP often starts insidiously, with malaise and nausea (q1).
  - Labs should be repeated at least every 12 hours, since platelets can fall about 40% a day (q2). The arithmetic is 78 x 0.6 = 46.8.
  - After delivery the platelet nadir comes at a mean of 23 hours, and the diagnosis is reassessed only if the picture is still worsening after day 4 (q3).
- pre-eclampsia-34 (key features 2, 4 and 4). This replaces the antepartum pulmonary edema case, which repeated s15 pre-eclampsia-09. The new case is severe pre-eclampsia in a marathon runner with a pulse of 46/minute.
  - q1 is a menu of first labs.
  - q2 teaches that ACOG PB 222 advises avoiding labetalol with bradycardia or heart block, so nifedipine is the key.
  - q3 teaches that magnesium given for eclampsia prevention before a planned birth at 31 weeks also reduces cerebral palsy. This follows SOGC No. 376 (2019), which I re-added as a source: RR 0.71 for any cerebral palsy, and it applies at 33+6 weeks or earlier.
- pre-eclampsia-37 (key features 1, 2 and 4). This replaces the unwitnessed eclamptic seizure and the magnesium question, which repeated s15 pre-eclampsia-06 and s15, s16 and s49. The new case is HELLP presenting as apparent preterm labour at 29 weeks.
  - q1: investigate before any tocolysis.
  - q2: HELLP diagnosed by the ACOG thresholds.
  - q3: a menu keying betamethasone and magnesium, with the tocolytics, high-dose dexamethasone and platelet transfusion as distractors.

### Single questions replaced

It now asks the reader to tell magnesium side effects (flushing, metallic taste, nausea and vomiting, palpitations) from toxicity (slurred speech), following ISSHP Table 9. - pre-eclampsia-38 q3. This replaces fetal bradycardia after a seizure, which repeated s15 pre-eclampsia-04 q4. It now teaches the mode of delivery. ACOG PB 222 says eclampsia by itself is not an indication for caesarean, and a stable woman progressing in labour can continue labour.
