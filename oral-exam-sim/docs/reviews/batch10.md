# Batch 10 clinical accuracy review

Adversarial review of the seven batch 10 cases (communication and systems). Every dose, threshold, choice label, legal statement and citation was checked. All cases keep `reviewed: false` until physician sign off.

## discrepancy-callback. A name on the discrepancy list

Verdict: Corrected

Changes
- Timeline and story. Before: on the fax path she "was stuck on the bathroom floor for an hour", which is a second fall. That clashed with the unsafe choice feedback that says there was no second fall. After: she could not get up from the toilet and needed help back to bed.
- Timeline. Before: the fax path said "Two hours later", so the call came after her 10:05 arrival. After: "An hour later".
- Return finding. Before: "Arrives by ambulance". On the clerk path Lucia drives her in. After: "Arrives at 10:05".
- Time to surgery. Before: "within 48 hours of the diagnosis". The Ontario quality standard counts 48 hours from first arrival at hospital. After: the model answer and teaching now say this, and point out that her first visit two nights ago means she is already close to the limit. This is a real teaching point for a missed fracture.
- Honesty about the counterfactual. Before: she "likely" would have had screw fixation. The evidence for screws versus hemiarthroplasty in a nondisplaced fracture at 78 is mixed, and the case itself tells the candidate not to speculate. After: "possibly a smaller operation", based on what the surgeon said.
- Sources. Before: the Canadian Disclosure Guidelines subtitle was given as "Being open with patients and families". After: the real title, "Being open and honest with patients and families", with a note that CPSI is now Healthcare Excellence Canada. Added verified URLs for the disclosure guidelines, the CMPA handbook and the two CPSO policies. The hip fracture standard is now cited as Health Quality Ontario, now Ontario Health, updated 2024, with no URL because the old PDF link redirects to a missing page.

Sources verified
- Apology Act, 2009, S.O. 2009, c. 3. https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/09a03 (also on CanLII)
- Canadian Disclosure Guidelines, 2011. https://www.healthcareexcellence.ca/resources/canadian-disclosure-guidelines/
- CMPA. Disclosing harm from healthcare delivery. https://www.cmpa-acpm.ca/en/advice-publications/browse-articles/2015/disclosing-harm-from-healthcare-delivery-open-and-honest-communication-with-patients

- CPSO. Medical Records Documentation. https://www.cpso.on.ca/Physicians/Policies-Guidance/Policies/Medical-Records-Documentation
- Hip fracture quality standard. Surgery within 48 hours of first presentation, and multimodal analgesia that considers peripheral nerve blocks, were confirmed from Ontario Health summaries.

Residual uncertainties
- The hip fracture standard does not clearly set out the CT or MRI rule for an occult fracture. Rubric dc-a2 cites it for that point. Consider a radiology or CAEP source.
- The ortho plan for surgery "tomorrow once apixaban has cleared" with a last dose at 08:00 and eGFR 50 is reasonable. Local DOAC timing protocols vary.

## highway-bus-collision. A call from the highway on a winter afternoon

Verdict: Corrected

Changes
- Triage logic. Before: patient four (the 7 year old) had no mental status at the door, so adult START would have made her red for not following commands, and the "adult rule would pass her" teaching did not hold. After: she is "drowsy but follows simple commands", so START makes her yellow and JumpSTART makes her red for a respiratory rate of 12.
- Age range. Before: the apneic child was 9 years old. JumpSTART is designed for children up to about 8. After: 8 years old, and the teaching says JumpSTART is for children up to about 8.
- Head injured child. Before: "hyperosmolar therapy if herniating". She already has a blown pupil and Cushing physiology. After: she is herniating now, give 3 percent saline 2 to 5 mL/kg, and do CT only if it does not delay transfer.
- Bleeding 14 year old. Before: straight to the OR. After: massive hemorrhage protocol first, then the OR because he is not settling.
- Tranexamic acid. Before: 15 mg/kg in children with no maximum or infusion. After: 1 g in adults, 15 mg/kg to a maximum of 1 g in children, each followed by an infusion.
- Numbers. Before: the end said 23 patients were treated. That did not account for the 11 self presenters. After: "More than thirty patients from the crash came through your department."
- Sources. ATLS updated from the 10th edition (2018) to the 11th edition (2025) with the ACS URL. The unverifiable "CAEP position statement on disaster preparedness" now cites Kollek D, et al. CAEP position statement. Hospital disaster preparedness. CJEM 2020, with its PubMed link. "IMS doctrine for Ontario" was replaced in 2021 by IMS Guidance Version 2.0, now cited with its URL. The OHA emergency codes citation was reworded plainly.

Sources verified
- ATLS 11th edition, launched 2025. https://www.facs.org/quality-programs/trauma/education/advanced-trauma-life-support/atls-11/
- CAEP hospital disaster preparedness, CJEM 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32425150/
- Ontario IMS Guidance Version 2.0. https://www.ontario.ca/document/incident-management-system-ims-guidance-version-2
- JumpSTART criteria (respiratory rate under 15 or over 45, 5 rescue breaths, age cut off about 8) confirmed from published training material. Benson 1996 (START) and Romig 2002 (JumpSTART) are real.
- OHA endorsement of standardized colour codes, including Code Orange, confirmed from Ontario hospital pages.

Residual uncertainties
- The O negative policy (children and females of childbearing potential) follows common Canadian practice. Local blood bank protocols set the age cut off.
- A community general surgeon doing damage control laparotomy on a 14 year old is realistic, but local pediatric surgery arrangements vary.

## crowded-resus-room. Too many voices in bay one

Verdict: Corrected

Changes
- Guideline currency. Before: the case cited the 2020 AHA guidelines and presented vector change and double sequential defibrillation as the expected next step. The 2025 AHA guidelines, adopted in the Heart and Stroke Canada edition, rate both as class 2b, may be considered after 3 or more failed shocks. They also note that DOSE VF showed benefit by intention to treat but not by treatment received. After: the feedback and teaching give DOSE VF results and the weak strength of the recommendation. The partial choice feedback now says standard shocks are still acceptable.
- Post arrest oxygen. Before: SpO2 92 to 98 percent (2020). After: 90 to 98 percent (2025 post cardiac arrest guideline).
- Temperature. Before: "prevent fever". After: deliberate temperature control between 32 and 37.5 degrees for the comatose patient, and prevent fever.
- Timeline. Before: the stem said 10:30 with the patient five minutes out, but he arrived at 10:30. After: 10:26 and four minutes out.
- Counts. Before: the 10:38 summary said 5 shocks and 3 doses of epinephrine, which does not match the drug times on every path. After: the recorder reads back the shock and epinephrine counts. Amiodarone 450 mg stays.
- Choice label. Before: raising the consultant's behaviour in front of the team was labelled unsafe. It is unprofessional but not a patient safety act. After: partial.
- Sources. Replaced Panchal 2020 with Wigginton JG, et al. Part 9. Adult advanced life support. 2025. Added Hirsch KG, et al. Part 11. Post cardiac arrest care. 2025, and moved the post arrest rubric items to it. Added the verified CPSO Professional Behaviour URL.

Checked and correct
- Amiodarone 300 mg then 150 mg, and holding the consultant's order for a further 300 mg after 450 mg.
- Epinephrine 1 mg every 3 to 5 minutes. Pauses under 10 seconds. Emergent angiography for STEMI after arrest regardless of coma.
- Cheskes 2022 (NEJM, DOSE VF), Carne 2012 and Hicks and Petrosoniak 2018 are real.

Sources verified
- 2025 AHA Part 9 and Part 11, Circulation 2025, DOI 10.1161/CIR.0000000000001376 and 10.1161/CIR.0000000000001375 (publisher site blocks automated fetches, so no URL was added). Class 2b wording confirmed through secondary summaries.
- Heart and Stroke Canada 2025 guideline edition released October 2025.
- CPSO. Professional Behaviour. https://www.cpso.on.ca/Physicians/Policies-Guidance/Policies/Physician-Behaviour-in-the-Professional-Environmen

Residual uncertainties
- The strong answer still includes a plan for double sequential shocks. That is defensible under a 2b recommendation but depends on local practice.
- Please check that the family presence rubric item is supported by Part 9 or needs the ethics part of the 2025 guidelines.

## Batch summary

All seven cases were corrected. None needed a rewrite. The most important fixes were these. The resuscitation case was out of date: vector change and double sequential defibrillation are now weak (class 2b) recommendations in the 2025 guidelines, and the post arrest oxygen and temperature targets have changed. The bus crash case had triage logic that did not work under START and JumpSTART as written, and a herniating child whose osmotherapy was left conditional. The hip fracture case missed that Ontario counts the 48 hour surgery clock from the first presentation. Several choice quality labels were changed so that unsafe means patient danger. ATLS citations were moved to the 11th edition. One CAEP citation could not be verified as written and was replaced with the real 2020 CJEM statement. No copied textbook or question bank text was found. Typecheck and case tests pass.
