## Passes with notes (no fix required)

- asthma-copd-49 q2: food allergy is a GINA Box 9-1 factor (verified). Food allergy appears in s04 accept lists, but this is now the only fatal-risk question that stays in the batch. Low-level overlap, for physician judgement. The phrase "His ICU admission ... already add to his risk" is loose. GINA Box 9-1 says "near-fatal asthma requiring intubation and mechanical ventilation", and Box 2-2 says "ICU admission, or mechanical ventilation". Acceptable.
- asthma-copd-49 q3 (writer flag, 2-day dexamethasone in adults). Adjudicated: the key holds. GINA 2024 text (read): "In adults, a small number of studies examined oral dexamethasone 12 to 16 mg given once daily for 1 to 2 days ... the relapse rate was similar to that with prednisolone for 3 to 5 days ... Oral dexamethasone should not be continued beyond 2 days because of concerns about metabolic side-effects." Prednisone 5 to 7 days is not an option, so only one answer is defensible. The 14-day and taper distractors are rejected with true reasons (GINA: 5 and 7 day courses as effective as 10 and 14 days, no benefit from tapering with ICS).
- asthma-copd-49 q4: GINA says poor perceivers may use regular PEF monitoring (verified) and lists nebulized SABA as a risk factor (verified).
- asthma-copd-50 q1, q2, q4: an adult dental-sedation aspiration labelled as new asthma is a new scenario. The bank's foreign body items are pediatric or a central airway tumour. Fixed focal wheeze is taught in s25 for an infant (localized air entry), so q1 is near but not the same decision.
- asthma-copd-52 q2: "Treat now and measure peak flow later" is new as a keyed decision and matches GINA ("before treatment if possible, without unduly delaying").
