## For the reviewers

- ATLS (37 questions) and Rosen's (20 questions) carry many items where no specific guideline exists. Neither text was read in this session, so the statements that rely on them need checking against the books: crush fluid strategy (44 q3), fat embolism management (30), posterior hip dislocation within 6 hours and post-reduction sciatic deficit (26), awake flexible scope intubation for laryngotracheal injury (27 q2), simple thoracostomy site and chest tube site (21 q2, 38 q2), thoracotomy criteria of 1500 mL and 200 mL/hour for 2 to 4 hours (38 q3), esmolol impulse control (47 q4), and the child's red cell dose of 10 mL/kg after one 20 mL/kg crystalloid bolus (22 q3).
- 20 q2 relies on the SOGC statement that indicated maternal imaging is not withheld or delayed because of the fetus. I recall this recommendation but did not read the guideline text.
- 20 q3 keys fibrinogen replacement at 1.8 g/L in a pregnant patient. SOGC lists fibrinogen below 200 mg/dL as an adverse factor, and the Ontario protocol replaces obstetric fibrinogen below 2.0 g/L. The patient is bleeding from a pelvic fracture, not an obstetric source. Check that this transfer is sound.
- 33 q4 cites the adult Brain Trauma Foundation guideline for a 3-year-old's end-tidal CO2 target. A pediatric specific source may be better.
- 36 q4 and 46 q3 test the Brain Trauma Foundation systolic floors (100 mmHg for ages 50 to 69, 110 mmHg for 15 to 49 or over 70). Rossaint gives a MAP of 80 mmHg or higher for severe TBI. Both are consistent but the options use the BTF values.
- 17 q2 keys PCC 2000 IU with vitamin K 10 mg IV. NAC says dosing should follow local policy and offers sample regimens. Both sample regimens give 2000 IU for this patient. Vitamin K 10 mg sits within the Rossaint 5 to 10 mg range.
- 19 applies Ontario law (the stem names Ontario). The keys follow HCCA s. 21 on prior capable wishes.
- 37 q1 names andexanet as a distractor. The NAC 2022 text says andexanet was not then approved by Health Canada. The explanation only states that it reverses factor Xa inhibitors.
- 23 cites ERC 2021. An ERC 2025 special circumstances chapter exists but could not be read here. Its traumatic arrest principles are not expected to change the keys.
- 42 q1 keys group AB plasma for an unknown recipient. Some Canadian sites now use group A plasma in trauma. Group A plasma is not an option, so no distractor becomes defensible.
- Web search quota ran out partway through. Later citations were verified through PubMed records rather than full text.
