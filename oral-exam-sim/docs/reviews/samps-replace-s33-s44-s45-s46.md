# SAMP replacement report: s33, s44, s45 and s46

Label: s33-s44-s45-s46. Files: src/samps/s33/dvt-pe.ts, src/samps/s44/multiple-patients.ts, src/samps/s45/multiple-trauma.ts and src/samps/s46/pediatric-fever.ts.

All 36 SAMPs listed for these batches in scratchpad/rebuild-list.json were rebuilt in place: 11 in s33, 2 in s44, 10 in s45 and 13 in s46. Each keeps its id, topic, question count and the key feature of every question in the same position. Each moves to version 2 with reviewed: false and a new neutral title. All questions are single or menu. The three FIX items in the triage files for SAMPs not being rebuilt were applied. No HOLD, READY or signed-off SAMP was changed.

## How scenarios were chosen

The whole bank (1 609 SAMPs) was dumped before writing, and again before finishing, and searched by scenario and by keyed decision, not only by wording, across all batches and topics. Related topics searched included chest-pain, sob, shock, airway, ems, burns, infectious-diseases, tox, seizures, headache, abdominal-pain and msk. Candidates dropped because the bank already holds them include CKD and contrast allergy with V/Q scanning, isolated subsegmental PE, HIT, IVC filters, mechanical valves, cancer with GI lesions, sickle cell fever, VP shunt fever, heat stroke, anticholinergic and serotonin toxicity, H2S in a manure pit, ammonia at a rink, CO during a power outage, lightning reverse triage, posterior sternoclavicular dislocation, facial fractures, epidural hematoma transfer, tension pneumothorax after intubation and blunt tamponade.

One rebuild collided with another writer working at the same time. The first draft of pediatric-fever-26 (nephrotic syndrome with fever and abdominal pain) matched infectious-diseases-29, which had just been rebuilt to version 2 as the same story. The final re-grep caught it, and pediatric-fever-26 was rebuilt a second time as meningitis in a teenager on infliximab and azathioprine.

In mock mode all questions of a SAMP show at once, so later updates were written so that they do not name or confirm an earlier key. For example, updates say "Testing confirms thrombus" or "The tests you ordered are normal" rather than naming the test that was keyed.
