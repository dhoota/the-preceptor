## For the physician

- asthma-copd-37 places an organophosphate poisoning under the asthma and COPD topic. In my judgement key feature 1 fits, because the case is a first wheezing episode wrongly labelled asthma at a walk-in clinic, and the feature asks exactly for the mimics to consider before accepting that label. Cholinergic bronchorrhea and bronchospasm belong on that list beside foreign body, heart failure, pulmonary embolism and anaphylaxis. The two key feature 7 questions are the weaker part of the claim. As drafted q2 tested antidote titration, which is not key feature 7 at all, so it was rebuilt as the respiratory support decision, and q3 tests the delayed muscle weakness that causes respiratory failure a day later. Both now sit inside the feature. If you would rather this case did not live in this topic at all, it should move to tox or sob rather than be patched further.
- asthma-copd-31 is outside the three coexisting problems named in key feature 4, as the writer says. The feature names heart failure, pneumonia and pneumothorax as examples, and all three are keyed by signed off cards, so the pool is used up. Allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis is a real coexisting problem in long-standing asthma, but it is a respirology diagnosis that an emergency physician suspects rather than confirms. Please decide whether you want it in this bank. If you keep it, note that q2 is keyed to the first test rather than to a confirmed diagnosis, which is the honest emergency department decision.
- asthma-copd-31 q3 keys high-flow nasal oxygen on the 2022 ERS guideline. That guideline's hypoxemic failure evidence comes mostly from pneumonia and similar disease, not from severe airway obstruction with mucus plugging. The key is still the best of the five options, since she has no hypercapnia and no indication for intubation, but the strength of the evidence behind it is worth your eye.
- asthma-copd-35 q3 sits under key feature 8, which asks for a confirmed recovery with objective measures, then inhaler technique and follow-up. The keyed decision is discharge advice about breastfeeding. The update carries the objective measure, a peak flow at 91% of her best an hour after treatment, and the explanation closes with technique and review in 2 to 7 days, so the feature is touched but not at the centre. If you read the feature narrowly, this question needs to change, and most objective recovery measures are already keyed elsewhere in the batch.
- asthma-copd-35 q2 was a near copy of asthma-copd-21 q4 before this review, same key string and an overlapping option set. The rebuild moves the discrimination onto this patient, a halved dose, an inhaled steroid, an intravenous steroid and delay until weaning. The key string prednisone 50 mg PO daily for 5 days still appears in both, because the GINA dose for an adult flare is what it is. If you want no repeated key string in the batch, the dose question in one of the two has to go.
- asthma-copd-41 q3 rests on a BTS and ICS good practice point, not a graded recommendation, and the guideline is British. There is no current Canadian guideline on drug delivery through a noninvasive ventilation circuit that I could find, and the stem earns the exception by showing she desaturates within a minute off the mask.
- asthma-copd-41 q2 keys starting bilevel ventilation before a two-hour transfer. The pH and PaCO2 threshold is guideline backed, but the timing before transport is practice reasoning rather than a graded recommendation, and transfer capability differs between services. Confirm that starting before the crew leaves is what you would teach.
