## For the reviewers

- burns-39 q3: Bier block (IV regional calcium) is also used for hand burns. It is not among the options, so the intra-arterial key stands, but check that no distractor reads as equivalent.
- burns-40: the systemic risk threshold of over 5% TBSA at any concentration is from McKee. The Canadian Antidote Guide gives a different trigger (over 1% with 50% or stronger acid). The stem gives both a large area and a falling calcium, so the key does not depend on which threshold is used.
- burns-41 q2: hydromorphone 2 mg IV as a first dose in a buprenorphine-maintained adult is a judgement call. The explanation says to titrate with monitoring.
- burns-42 q4 cites the ABA pain guideline for analgesia-first sedation in a ventilated patient. The guideline was confirmed to exist but its full text was not opened.
- burns-43 q2: the point that lid swelling makes later eye examination harder is general clinical knowledge, not a quoted line from the cited source.
