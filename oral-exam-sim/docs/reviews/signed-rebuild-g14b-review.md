## Sources (opened)

- Conde-Agudelo 2020, AJOG (PMC OAI full text). It confirms the diagnostic criteria, the roughly 50% accuracy figure and ampicillin 2 g q6h with gentamicin 5 mg/kg q24h. It says: "In penicillin-allergic patients, clindamycin 900 mg IV every 8 hours or vancomycin 1 g IV every 12 hours or erythromycin ... can be used instead of ampicillin." It also confirms intrapartum over postpartum treatment (neonatal pneumonia or sepsis 0% vs 31.6%), acetaminophen, "not an indication for cesarean delivery", and that the interval from diagnosis to delivery does not change outcomes. The DOI is 10.1016/j.ajog.2020.09.044.
- CPS, Jefferies 2017 (cps.ca, posted 15 June 2017). It confirms the five risk factors, that they are additive, and that adequate prophylaxis is penicillin G, ampicillin or cefazolin given 4 hours or more before birth. It also says that clindamycin or vancomycin prophylaxis "should be considered inadequate IAP when managing the neonate", and that risk-based prophylaxis applies "if GBS status is unknown".
- Manitoba protocol, August 2016. The live PDF is byte-identical to the writer's copy. It confirms every q4 indication, the caesarean exception, the penicillin G regimen, the cefazolin exclusion for anaphylaxis, angioedema, respiratory distress or urticaria, and vancomycin 1 g q12h when susceptibility is unknown.

## For the physician

- **infectious-diseases-02 fails review on q1 scoring, not on its medicine.** Vancomycin or clindamycin plus gentamicin for intraamniotic infection after penicillin anaphylaxis is the regimen the source gives. The accept list lets a candidate score full marks without gentamicin, or without any gram positive agent.
- **Vancomycin dose.** The key uses 1 g IV q12h, from the 2020 AJOG review and the 2016 Manitoba protocol. At 78 kg that is about 13 mg/kg. More recent prophylaxis guidance (ACOG 2020) moved to 20 mg/kg q8h, up to 2 g. Scoring accepts any vancomycin dose, so this does not change marks. Please confirm which dose the explanation should state.
- **q4 option 0, fever with a negative swab, is Manitoba-specific.** It is not national. Please confirm you want a provincial protocol tested, or ask for option 0 to be replaced.
- **q3 "no prophylaxis".** It reads correctly as a risk factor only if phrased as "no adequate prophylaxis", because vancomycin never counts as adequate for the newborn. The 4 cm dilation does not change this.

Raising `required` to 3 would make the other factors (rupture of membranes of 18 hours or more, fever, intraamniotic infection) count. That is a scoring change for the writer, not a reviewer fix.
