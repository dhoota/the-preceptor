## For the physician

- 27 q1: No aortic stenosis sedation guideline could be opened in full text. ASA 2018 supports cardiovascular disease as a risk factor for sedation hypotension. The fixed-output physiology in severe aortic stenosis is stated from standard physiology.
- 32 q2: ASA OSA 2014 supports capnography and nonsupine positioning. It does not name preoxygenation. ASA 2018 recommends supplemental oxygen. The claim that a large total-weight propofol bolus is unsafe is pharmacology and has no specific citation.
- 32 q4: A jaw thrust with a nasal airway as the first step is standard airway practice. The cited OSA guideline does not spell it out.
- 33 q2: The flumazenil monograph supports avoiding flumazenil. The naloxone starting dose of 0.04 mg is not in that monograph. Kim HK and Nelson LS (Expert Opin Drug Saf. 2015) recommend 0.04 mg, but for opioid-dependent patients. ASA 2018 warns that abrupt opioid reversal causes pain, hypertension and pulmonary edema. Please confirm the 0.04 mg key.
- 36 q2: The current BOAST (updated 2025) says "limb elevated" and gives no height. The 2016 version said "heart level". A distractor was replaced so that the question no longer depends on this point. The current BOAST asks for a joint decision with the surgeon before a regional block. It does not ban one.
- 37 q1 and 45 q3: Davison does not discuss meperidine. The AGS Beers Criteria 2023 advise avoiding meperidine for neurotoxicity. It is not cited here, because there was no spare question to cite it.
- 39 q1: Wertheimer A et al. (Injury. 2018) found femoral shaft fractures were not more often transfused in the first 48 hours. They advise looking for other bleeding sources in shock. The explanation now says possible hemorrhage and does not claim a fixed thigh volume.
- 24 q3: The reasons for rejecting bed rest and zopiclone are general. Davison does not state them.
- 17 q3: The claim that ketamine 1.5 mg/kg is a dissociative dose comes from the Green 2011 guideline. That guideline is not in this SAMP's source list.
- 46 q1: Ciavola is a pediatric review, and the patient is 17.
- Roberts and Hedges, another textbook, is still cited in several of these SAMPs. It was outside this task.
